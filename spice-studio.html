Tips for IC designers

• Put a small load capacitor on every digital node — real gates always see fan-out load, and oscillators need .ic to break symmetry (a perfect ring at its metastable point is a valid DC solution!).
• Node names are case-insensitive; ground is always 0.
• Pick tstep at least 10× smaller than the fastest edge you care about.

Why this tool exists

Every chip on earth is designed with EDA software dominated by three foreign companies — Synopsys, Cadence and Siemens EDA — holding over 85% of a ~$16B market. Commercial analog flows (Cadence Virtuoso + Spectre class) cost hundreds of thousands of dollars per seat per year, which prices out most Indian startups, MSMEs and college labs.

India now designs thousands of chips a year (about 20% of the world's chip designers work here), has 100+ fabless startups using the government's shared EDA grid, and its first commercial fab (Tata–PSMC, Dholera) and OSAT plants (CG Semi, Kaynes, Micron ATMP) are coming online. The need for cheap, quality, locally-supported design software has never been clearer.

SPICE Studio is a demonstration of the lowest-hanging fruit in that gap: a zero-install, zero-cost SPICE-class simulator that any engineer or student can open in a browser — for circuit homework, quick what-if checks, analog IP bring-up planning, and interview/hiring skill tests — before touching an expensive licensed tool.

Engineering notes

The engine implements Modified Nodal Analysis with sparse-aware dense LU, Newton–Raphson with junction limiting and gmin stepping, trapezoidal companion models, breakpoint-aware adaptive time stepping, and hierarchical subcircuit expansion. It is ~700 lines of dependency-free JavaScript — auditable, hackable, embeddable. Verified against analytic solutions for RC/RLC/diode/MOS testbenches.

This prototype is for education and design exploration. Sign-off simulation should use golden licensed tools with foundry-certified models.
